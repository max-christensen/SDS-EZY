What SDS EZY does

SDS EZY is a register of the chemicals at your workplace. For each chemical it keeps the safety data sheet (SDS), the hazard details, where it is stored, and a one-page hazard risk assessment.

When you upload an SDS, the app reads it, adds the chemical to the register and drafts the risk assessment for you. A person then checks the draft. It also tells you when an SDS is more than 5 years old and needs replacing.

Logging in

Go to the app's web address and log in with the email and password you were given. There is no self-signup. An admin adds each person under Users.

If your business has switched on emailed codes, you also get a 6-digit code by email after your password. Type it in to finish. The code works for 10 minutes. You stay logged in for 30 days, so you only see the code about once a month on each device.

If the code email does not arrive, check your junk folder, then ask your admin to check the email setup.

The review list

This is the home screen. It shows what needs attention, with a count for each:

  • Overdue: the SDS is more than 5 years old. Get a new one from the supplier.
  • Due soon: the SDS will be 5 years old within 90 days.
  • No SDS: no safety data sheet is attached yet.
  • Not checked yet: nobody has decided if the chemical is hazardous.

Tap a row to open the chemical. The review date is the issue date on the SDS plus 5 years.

Uploading an SDS

Choose Upload SDS, pick the PDF, and answer a few short questions about how the chemical is used (where, how much, how long people are exposed, how well ventilated). The app reads the PDF, adds the chemical and drafts the assessment.

  • Only PDF files are accepted, up to the size limit set in Settings.
  • If the same file is already on record, the app tells you. If it looks like a newer version of an SDS you already have, you can add it as the new version. The old one is kept in the history.
  • If AI is not set up, or it fails, the SDS and the chemical are still saved. The assessment is left blank for an admin to fill in.
  • Anything the AI reported that cannot be found in the PDF text is left blank and marked Not found in the SDS text, check.

The register

Every chemical, one per row. On a phone each chemical is a card. Use the search box to find a chemical by name, supplier, product code or CAS number. Use the buttons under it to filter (for example, only those overdue), and the sort menu to change the order.

Print makes a clean printed copy of what you can see. Download CSV saves it as a spreadsheet file.

The hazardous register

Only the chemicals decided as hazardous, with their hazard pictograms, signal word and dangerous goods class. Print it for your records.

A chemical's page

Shows the product details, the hazard information from the SDS (with the standard red-diamond hazard pictograms), the SDS itself, the risk assessment and where it is stored.

  • View the PDF opens the safety data sheet in a new tab.
  • Also covers N other products lists other products that share the same SDS.
  • Version history lists earlier versions of the SDS.
  • Print assessment makes a clean one-page copy, with your business name at the top.

If a chemical has been checked and is not hazardous, the page says so and does not ask for an assessment.

The risk assessment

The assessment is laid out like the one-page checklist: the product, the supplier and phone, the dates, the form and use, how the chemical can get in (inhalation, ingestion, absorption), fire and spill response, the controls (isolation, ventilation, other), PPE, the level of risk, whether air monitoring or health surveillance is needed, and who assessed it and when.

The level of risk is one of: Not significant, Significant but effectively controlled, Significant and not effectively controlled, or Uncertain. It starts as Uncertain unless the SDS and the answers clearly show something else.

A draft is only a draft. Assessments marked Prepared from the SDS were drafted by the app. Read them, compare them with the SDS and your own workplace, and fix anything that is wrong.

Editing (admins)

Admins see an Edit button on each chemical. The edit screen has separate sections, each saved on its own:

  • Product details and decision: names, supplier, use, and whether it is hazardous.
  • Safety data sheet: attach one, fix its details, or replace it with a newer version. Replacing keeps the old one in the history and moves every product that used it to the new one. Your assessment is not changed, so check it against the new SDS.
  • Risk assessment: fill in or correct every part. Save changes updates the current assessment. Save as a new version keeps the old one in the history.
  • Storage locations: where it is kept and how much, in litres or kilograms.

The tick boxes for controls and PPE come from Options, where you can add, rename, reorder and switch off choices.

QR labels

Each chemical has a QR label you can print and stick on the container or cabinet. Scanning it with a phone opens that chemical's page (after logging in).

The manifest

The manifest lists the hazardous chemicals that have a storage location, grouped by dangerous goods class, with the total litres and kilograms for each class. Use it to see if you are near a storage limit.

Take-away copies

The Take-away screen makes copies of the register that you can keep without logging in: spreadsheets, a PDF, and a zip with the SDS files. The disclaimer is included in each one. Make a new copy now and then, and keep it somewhere safe.

Importing a spreadsheet (admins)

If you already keep a chemical list in a spreadsheet, Import reads it, lets you match its columns to ours, and adds the chemicals and their storage locations. Chemicals that are already there (same name and supplier) are skipped. Then upload the SDS for each one.

Settings and AI (admins)

  • Business details: name, address, phone, email and ABN. They appear on printed pages.
  • Colours and logo: pick a preset or your own colours. The app checks that text stays readable. The logo shows next to your business name.
  • Uploads: the biggest file allowed and how many uploads each person can make per hour.
  • Sign-in security: the emailed login code, once your host has set up email.
  • Reading SDSs with AI: choose a provider (Cloudflare Workers AI needs no key; Claude, OpenAI and Gemini need your own key) and optionally a model. The key is stored encrypted and is never shown again. Press Test the saved settings to check it works. If your host set the key as a server secret, that one is used.

Using it on your phone

SDS EZY is made to work at the chemical cabinet. On a phone, the menu is behind the Menu button, the register is a list of cards, and everything is sized for fingers. Scan a QR label to jump straight to a chemical, then tap View the PDF to read the SDS.
